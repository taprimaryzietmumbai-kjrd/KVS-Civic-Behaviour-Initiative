Workshop purpose

Make civic behaviour part of everyday school life

Use familiar lessons and school routines to strengthen care, respect, cooperation and responsible use of shared spaces—without creating a separate subject or extra workload.

Learning journey
Grades 1–10

Four developmental stages

Foundational · 1–2 Preparatory · 3–5 Middle · 6–8 Secondary · 9–10
Core sequence

Observe → Connect → Model → Practise → Reflect

Start with an observable behaviour, link it to existing curriculum, model it consistently and reinforce it through routine.

What participants will do

  • Distinguish awareness from observable behaviour.
  • Use handbook guidance to plan one grade-linked experience.
  • Practise responding to a student-raised concern.
  • Draft a feasible first action for their KV.

Non-negotiable safeguards

  • No shaming, punishment, policing, ranking or competitions.
  • Do not assign hazardous, technical or adult cleaning work to children.
  • Respect dignity, inclusion and the role of staff who maintain spaces.
  • Use existing reporting channels and close the loop with students.

Workshop Presentation

School Response Process

Presentation on responding to student-reported cleanliness and civic behaviour concerns, from reporting through follow-up and closure.

Open School Response Process ↗
Part 1

Common across all grades

Principal and teacher guidance, adult modelling, readiness and the school response process.

Part 2

Common across stages

Classroom observation and stage-appropriate student feedback. These support learning; they are not inspection or scoring tools.

Part 3

Grade-wise components

Curriculum links, core activity, teacher guidance, repeated practices, participation, reflection and relevant reinforcement.

Learning focus by stage

Grades 1–2 · Foundational

Care for self, belongings and immediate surroundings through simple everyday practices.

Grades 3–5 · Preparatory

Care for shared spaces and resources; cooperation and consideration for others.

Grades 6–8 · Middle

Observe situations, understand systems and roles, practise safely and know when adult support is needed.

Grades 9–10 · Secondary

Use evidence, examine perspectives and systems, and take bounded civic action within student responsibilities.

When a student raises a concern

1
Share concern
Make it safe and clear for students to speak.
2
Understand concern
Listen and establish what support is needed.
3
Respond
Use the appropriate existing school channel.
4
Close the loop
Tell students what happened, as appropriate.
Remember: Children can observe, suggest and practise safe habits. Adults remain responsible for hazards, technical work, institutional decisions and ensuring concerns receive a response.

Activity 1 · 15–20 minutes

Civic sense or civic behaviour?

Task: Distinguish the internal civic sense mindset from observable civic behaviour. Then use the table below to identify the mindset, the desired action, the opposite unwanted action, and the everyday Indian context.

No.Civic Sense MindsetDesired Civic Behaviour

(What we should avoid)		Common Indian Context

2Public property belongs to all of us.
3Every person deserves dignity and respect.
4Road safety is a shared responsibility.
5Everyone deserves a fair turn.
6Water and electricity are precious resources.
7Shared spaces require consideration for others.
8The environment must be protected.
9Rules help community life function safely and fairly.

Group task: complete the three blank columns. Focus on observable actions and realistic everyday Indian settings; discuss without blaming individuals.

Complete the table and group details before submitting. One submission records all 10 rows and the group's key insight.

Activity 2 · 20–30 minutes

School walkthrough: observe without judging

Choose a shared space (classroom, corridor, playground, water point). Describe the behaviour and conditions around it; avoid naming or blaming individuals.

Activity 3 · 25 minutes

Handbook-to-classroom challenge

Select one grade, connect a current curriculum lesson, then plan one safe, repeatable behaviour and an existing routine that can reinforce it.

Activity 4 · 15 minutes

From concern to closure

Scenario: Students report that a shared water point is often left wet and slippery. What is the responsible sequence?

Choose the best response.

Workshop Activity · Adult Modelling · 40–50 minutes

Case Studies: Children Learn What Adults Practise

Choose a case study, select your workshop role and name from the existing role roster, then record your group's recommendations. Discuss the case without blaming individuals. Use the recommendations to identify adult behaviours that can become part of everyday school routines.

Recommendations should focus on observable adult actions, dignity, safety and follow-through. Avoid punishment, public shaming, ranking or blaming individuals.
